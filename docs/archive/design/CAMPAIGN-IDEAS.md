# Ideas that make the app worth trying

These are testable promises, not claims that the product already delivers them. The concepts use fictional records. The first images compare four design directions with identical financial content and headline. After choosing a credible visual system, vary the promise and the demonstrated moment.

## The first campaign image

**Know what’s free to spend.**

Accounts, commitments, and your next decision.

The image must do more than display an attractive $600. It must show that $5,000 is available, $4,400 is reserved, and $600 is free. That relationship is the product idea. A next screen shows a $120 purchase preview and $480 free afterward. The person should understand the benefit without reading an accounting explanation.

CTA: **Explore the concept**. Disclosure: **Concept preview · Not yet available**.

This is the strongest starting hypothesis in our existing concept, not a finding about actual demand. It gives each design agent the same useful job to express.

## Eight specific promises to explore

| Promise | Person and moment | What the creative should show | Why they might care | What we need to learn |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Know what’s free to spend.** | A person about to make a discretionary purchase | $5,000 available → $4,400 reserved → $600 free; a $120 preview leaves $480 | Converts several balances and plans into a usable decision | Do people understand and want this distinction? |
| **Three accounts. One clear answer.** | Someone switching between bank apps and a spreadsheet | Three clearly named Account rows and a reconciled combined view | Reduces the work of assembling the picture | Is the pain seeing the picture, keeping it current, or both? |
| **Know what’s covered. Enjoy what’s left.** | A household planning an ordinary month | Rent, groceries, bills, and an emergency fund visibly reserved, with the Free amount separate | Puts enjoyment and commitments in the same plan | Does this feel useful and reassuring without implying a guarantee? |
| **A money plan you can both see.** | Two people who share some spending | One Book, the same Reserve values, and a small attributed planning change | Makes shared intent visible | Do they want joint planning, expense splitting, or individual privacy controls? |
| **Check the purchase before you make it.** | Someone weighing a weekend booking or household item | A focused amount input changing the preview from $600 to $480 | Gives the user an immediate reason to open the app | Does this answer a real question often enough to matter? |
| **Less catching up. More knowing.** | Someone whose current tracker is always behind | A proposed imported record next to its matching manual record; one reviewed result | Shows the benefit of import and matching without a magical AI claim | Is automation important enough to overcome setup and trust concerns? |
| **Make room for the things you want.** | A person balancing everyday life with a goal | An explicit Reserve change with a corresponding Free amount change | Turns a goal into a visible tradeoff | Do people prefer optional Reserves or a complete envelope ritual? |
| **Your money plan travels with you.** | Someone recording a purchase with no connection | A saved local expense and a separate pending-sync state | Shows a concrete benefit of local availability | Is offline use a buying reason, or a quality people expect? |

No promise here means guaranteed savings, no stress, universal bank coverage, or financial advice. Prefer concrete screen evidence to those claims.

## How the four directions can express the same benefit

| Moment | Clarity | Daylight | Horizon | Tactile |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Free amount | A decisive figure with an exact reconciliation | A calm daily answer with a human context line | An illuminated amount above a labelled allocation arc | A bold amount beside one purposeful dimensional form |
| Covered commitments | A precise ledger with aligned values | A dated, readable plan with subtle markers | A quiet sequence along the month, with exact Reserve rows | Compact physical-feeling Reserve tiles with visible amounts |
| Household plan | Clear Book identity and attributed change | Two understated people cues and shared planning language | One coherent shared view, with ownership stated in text | The same Reserve objects and a traceable change |
| Purchase check | A confident calculator-like input | A comfortable decision sheet with plain language | A restrained shift in the illuminated allocation state | A large amount control and a clear before/after result |
| Record review | Source and match shown in aligned rows | A brief review step that explains what needs attention | A focused exception panel against a quiet background | One deliberate confirm action with an understandable result |
| Monthly report | A beautifully typeset financial document | An editorial recap with a humane tone | Legible charts whose scales and dates are visible | A strong graphic summary built from real labelled amounts |

These are 24 scene/style combinations. They are a production map, not a request to generate all of them now. The current eight images establish whether each system is worth developing.

## First follow-on creative batch

Once a direction is chosen, make three ads from that same system:

1. **Purchase decision:** Overview → enter $120 → $480 free afterward. Use “Check the purchase before you make it.”
2. **Covered month:** Reveal the four funded Reserves and the separate Free amount. Use “Know what’s covered. Enjoy what’s left.”
3. **Current picture:** Bring three Account balances into one reconciled view. Use “Three accounts. One clear answer.”

The second and third ideas need different screen composition, not just replacement headlines. The same app typography, controls, and financial meaning must carry through all three.

A later short video can animate those real transitions. Do not simply move static phones around while claiming to demonstrate an app. Reserve an attributed-record review sequence for the automation hypothesis once its screens have been designed.

## How to use the first results

First assess whether the screens look like a coherent product and communicate the common scenario. The founder's visual choice is a design decision, not market validation.

For a later demand experiment, keep audience, promise, destination, offer, and placement comparable when testing visual direction. Once the direction is stable, hold it constant while testing promises. If design and promise change together, the result describes that combined concept and cannot identify which part helped.

The review images are not permission to publish. Before a campaign uses them, inspect financial text at delivery size, rebuild or correct any generated defects, make the matching concept page, and confirm the actual channel, budget, and offer. Existing demand-kit measurement guidance still applies; no advertising account or spend is used in this task.
