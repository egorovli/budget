## Art direction

Create a quiet, exceptionally polished native financial tool. The energy comes from typographic hierarchy, aligned numbers, precise spacing, and one vivid cobalt action color. Chalk-white and very light cool grey, near-black ink, a fine grey divider. Avoid beige, olive, soft mascots, and decorative gradients. Think legibility under time pressure, with a strong confident $600 and a clear smaller reconciliation directly beneath it. Use structured rows instead of enclosing everything in cards. Top Book switcher, restrained Account strip, compact transaction preview; a consistent three-item bottom navigation (Overview, Activity, Plan) and add action.

Overview should feel like an information instrument: one decisive number, small meaningful status, coherent hierarchy. Plan should feel like a beautifully typeset ledger with restrained progress marks. Purchase check should feel like a precise calculator that instantly explains the result. Standard-looking controls can be beautiful through proportion and focus. Typography should be neutral, sharp, and carefully weighted, with tabular figures in lists.

The ad should be light, editorial, and highly legible. A large straight phone dominates; cropped blue typographic or line accents may give identity without turning into decorative finance clip art. Promise territory: confidence before the next purchase. Secondary future hooks: “Three accounts. One clear answer.” and “Every total has a trail.” Do not add those to the required matched ad.

Distinctness test: this must be the crispest, most restrained and professional of the four. No space scenes, illustrated characters, clay objects, or warm stationery.
