# What makes these apps worth studying

Research date: 12 September 2026. Scope: all 41 non-game winner entries in Apple's 2020–2026 Design Awards, plus eight selected finalists. This is a design reference set, not a ranking of business success.

The useful lesson is that a strong app has a clear point of view, a coherent visual system, and controls that suit its main task. A polished advertisement should show that product experience. A phone frame, attractive gradient, and large number do not establish the experience by themselves.

Apple's award pages establish the award facts below. Apple's interviews provide evidence about design intent and process. The proposed applications to Sort of Budget are our interpretation. They are not Apple recommendations, conversion evidence, or results from testing these apps ourselves. Static references cannot establish speed, gesture quality, accessibility, reliability, or current behavior.

## Start with these different directions

These are directions to compare, not an instruction to combine every style.

| Direction | First references to inspect | What to decide for Sort of Budget |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Precise, calm financial instrument | Flighty, Halide, Darkroom; Copilot Money finalist | Can the user identify the relevant amount and its state before reading an explanation? |
| Warm household companion | Gentler Streak, Headspace, Bears Gratitude | How much warmth should surround a serious financial answer? |
| Beautiful data as the main attraction | Tide Guide, Any Distance; Lumy and Sunlitt finalists | Can an expressive chart improve understanding without making values ambiguous? |
| Fast, practical daily tool | Crouton; Mela, iA Writer, Structured, Arc Search finalists | Can entry, review, and planning feel complete with very few visible controls? |
| Distinctive, tactile object | (Not Boring) Habits, Looom, grug | Would one memorable interaction create a useful identity for Sort of Budget? |
| Content or workspace first | Procreate, Feather, Shapr 3D, Play | How much interface can disappear while the user's work stays clear? |

Start by selecting whole app experiences that appeal to you. Then identify the particular parts: typography, density, color, chart style, navigation, illustration, or motion. A preference for one screen does not require adopting that app's entire personality.

## Eight deeper lessons from the designers

### Flighty: make the current situation obvious

Apple's interview describes a team that borrows established airport information conventions, makes key details continuously available, and plans for loss of network access during a flight. Its concept process includes 20 design ideas to resist the temptation to assemble something from the existing asset library. This is evidence of deliberate exploration, not evidence that exactly 20 concepts is a universal rule. [Behind the Design: Flighty](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=970ncww4).

**Sort of Budget interpretation:** Make a meaningful financial situation the center of the design: the selected Book, the Free amount, the next commitment, and anything that needs review. The appearance must remain coherent when sync is stale. The useful reference is the clarity of state, not an airport theme.

**Tradeoff to test:** A large headline amount can feel decisive while concealing assumptions. Its label, date, currency, and path to the source records must make its meaning clear.

### Halide: professional depth without a crowded first screen

Halide's designers describe consistent gestures and controls that preserve muscle memory. A limited highlight color indicates active features, but the team also learned that color alone can fail to explain state. Their aim was a camera that felt approachable while retaining advanced tools. [Behind the Design: Halide Mark II](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=x6bv1a36).

**Sort of Budget interpretation:** Give amount entry a precise, comfortable control surface. Reveal optional attribution and advanced reporting when requested. Use a stable position and clear label for the main action. Add text or shape to state cues.

**Tradeoff to test:** Hidden controls can create an attractive screenshot and a poor first-use experience. Show an obvious path to every essential action before adding gesture shortcuts.

### Darkroom: remove repeated work across the whole task

Darkroom's founder describes frustration with moving through several apps to finish one image. The team studied taps per action and organized direct photo access, previews, and saving around a complete workflow. The interface follows that purpose. [Behind the Design: Darkroom](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=nxnjx66e).

**Sort of Budget interpretation:** Prototype the entire sequence from new record to checked result. For example: inspect a suggested match, accept it, return to Activity, and see the correct Account balance. The record must not appear twice. This gives a video an actual story.

**Tradeoff to test:** Fewer taps are useful only when the result stays clear and recoverable. A fast action that silently changes a financial meaning is a failure.

### Gentler Streak: help people interpret their own situation

The team describes an encouraging experience that considers the user's current capacity, explains statistics, and treats recovery as valid. Apple also reports that the initial concept had useful return behavior before the final app, but does not provide enough numbers for a demand benchmark. [Behind the Design: Gentler Streak](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=3m0ht22s).

**Sort of Budget interpretation:** A household finance app can feel composed and humane during a difficult month. Explain a shortfall and show possible next actions. Avoid language that treats a spending Category as a moral judgment.

**Tradeoff to test:** Friendly wording cannot soften away a real negative Free amount. Precision and warmth must appear together.

### Headspace: simple surface, useful depth

Apple's interview describes a move toward a Today view with timely activities, while an Explore area holds the deeper collection. Recognizable illustration and purpose-based language help users approach a complex subject. [Behind the Design: Headspace](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=fkfnhq8u).

**Sort of Budget interpretation:** A daily view can answer the user's immediate questions while reports and detailed history remain accessible. Illustration could support onboarding, empty states, and reassurance rather than fill every financial screen.

**Tradeoff to test:** A companion style may appeal to one household and feel childish to another. Compare it with a quieter direction using the same financial scenario.

### Any Distance: a recognizable artifact can attract attention

The founder describes early social templates that caused people to ask which app had made them. The eventual app presents data in distinctive formats and provides controls over which metrics people share. This is a useful origin story, not a controlled experiment proving visual style caused demand. [Behind the Design: Any Distance](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=uiiopcl8).

**Sort of Budget interpretation:** A well-designed monthly summary can become an excellent campaign artifact. It should arise from the same visual system as the app. Use synthetic data for demonstrations; sharing a real financial summary would need an explicit user choice.

**Tradeoff to test:** Financial disclosure has a different cost from workout sharing. Attractive summary cards do not imply people want a social feed.

### (Not Boring) Habits: commit to a particular personality

The designers describe a deliberate, expressive completion interaction built from animation, sound, and haptics. They also explain that familiar system controls remain underneath the visual identity and that the style is aimed at a particular audience. [Behind the Design: (Not Boring) Habits](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=9ab1g4r3).

**Sort of Budget interpretation:** Explore one memorable interaction: perhaps moving money into a Reserve or finishing a review. Let it express a real action and outcome.

**Tradeoff to test:** A satisfying animation repeated dozens of times can become a delay. Evaluate the quiet and reduced-motion versions alongside the expressive one. Do not turn spending into a celebration by default.

### Crouton: organize around the moment of use

The developer describes collecting recipes from varied sources and keeping the app's core intentions stable as it grows. His design concern is how the parts connect into a useful path. Apple's related interview summary highlights showing only the information needed for the cooking step. [Behind the Design: Crouton](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=9x75y43e), [Apple's November 2024 interview summary](https://developer.apple.com/hello/november24/).

**Sort of Budget interpretation:** A transaction review, a purchase check, and a household planning session are different moments. Each can have a focused screen while retaining one design language.

**Tradeoff to test:** Too many separate modes can fragment the product. Check whether the person knows where they are and can return to the relevant Book and records.

## What to inspect in every reference

This is an evaluation method for our review, not a set of properties verified in every winning app.

1. **Hierarchy:** What do you notice first, second, and third? Does that order match the person's task?
2. **Typography:** How do type size, weight, numerals, line length, and spacing separate information? Can the smallest meaningful label be read at normal phone size?
3. **Density:** How much useful information fits without competing for attention? Which details appear only when needed?
4. **Color:** Does color identify something, communicate a state, or establish a mood? Is the meaning still clear without color?
5. **Shape and material:** Are cards, borders, shadows, and surfaces used consistently? Does each layer explain a real relationship?
6. **Navigation:** Is the next action apparent? Are repeated actions in stable positions? Can someone discover the action without knowing a gesture?
7. **Data:** Are labels, dates, units, scales, comparisons, and source states visible? Is the chart both attractive and useful?
8. **Motion:** What changes, why does it change, and where does it go? Stills can suggest this question; they cannot answer it.
9. **Personality:** Which detail would still identify the app if the logo were removed? Is that detail connected to its purpose?
10. **Continuity:** Do entry, detail, list, summary, empty, error, and offline states feel like parts of the same app?

## How these references should guide the next design step

First, collect the user's picks and dislikes with a short reason for each. Keep contradictory preferences visible: liking a warm palette does not necessarily mean wanting a mascot, and liking a dense report does not mean wanting a dense home screen.

Next, produce three different Sort of Budget design directions with the **same** content and scenario. Each direction should include a Book overview, Activity, fast transaction entry, and a purchase or Reserve decision. Use consistent, correct sample amounts. Give each direction enough depth to judge the experience rather than only its hero screen.

Then produce one short interactive sequence for the preferred direction. Check its ordinary and difficult states: an empty Book, a negative Free amount, a long Counterparty name, large text, a missing source update, and offline entry. The project's existing distinction between Account balance, Available money, Reserve, Free amount, and Net worth must remain visible in the design. [Sort of Budget principles](../../../principles.md), [Sort of Budget domain language](../../../../GLOSSARY.md).

Only after that should campaign variations reuse the approved app surfaces. Change the promise and scenario while preserving the product identity. If the design direction is still being tested, label it as a design experiment instead of treating visual differences as evidence about the product promise alone.

## Evidence limits

- This set is complete for the stated non-game winner years, not for the full history of Apple awards or all excellent apps.
- Apple selects for its own award criteria and platform ecosystem. That selection is useful for craftsmanship research but has selection bias.
- Apple interviews are primary evidence of what Apple and the creators say. They remain editorial and promotional sources.
- Historical award images and current App Store images can show different product versions. Preserve their source and date in the library.
- The awarded platform matters. An iPhone screenshot of a multi-platform product does not establish why a visionOS experience won.
- Awards and attractive screenshots do not establish profitability, retention, willingness to pay, or suitability for Sort of Budget. Those require separate product evidence.
