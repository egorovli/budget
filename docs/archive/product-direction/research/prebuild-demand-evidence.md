# Evidence before building the finance application

Researched: 2026-09-12

Question: What can ads, concept pages, waitlists, and prototypes establish before the product exists?

## Finding

These tests can reduce uncertainty before a large build. Each test supports a narrow conclusion about a defined audience, offer, and requested action. A successful campaign is a reason to conduct the next test; it does not establish lasting demand for a working finance application. This is our synthesis of the methods below, not evidence that Budgetly has demand.

## What the sources establish

Strategyzer distinguishes reactions to an artifact, low-effort actions, meaningful commitments, and real use. A signup is behavior, but a commitment of time or money can supply stronger evidence for the corresponding assumption. The source also warns that evidence from users does not automatically describe the people who make purchase decisions. Its scale is a practitioner framework, not a measured probability of startup success. [Strategyzer: evidence strength](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/business-project-succcess)

The UK Government Digital Service uses prototypes to test designs before implementation. It distinguishes sketches from code prototypes that support realistic interaction. Prototype code need not meet production security or performance standards, so a successful prototype does not establish production readiness. This is service-design guidance, not a consumer-finance demand study. [GDS: making prototypes](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/design/making-prototypes)

Microsoft's experimentation team recommends a simple, measurable hypothesis, suitable metrics, sufficient sample size, and attention to the population tested. It explicitly warns against transferring results from an internal or beta audience directly to the general population. These are controlled-experiment practices; running two ordinary ad campaigns does not by itself create a controlled comparison. [Microsoft: pre-experiment practices](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/articles/patterns-of-trustworthy-experimentation-pre-experiment-stage/)

Strategyzer's Test Card asks the team to specify the hypothesis, test, measurement, and success threshold as part of test design. This supports deciding what the evidence must show before seeing the result. It provides no universal conversion threshold for this product. [Strategyzer: Test Card](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/validate-your-ideas-with-the-test-card)

## Application to this product — our inference

| Test | Useful evidence | Conclusion it cannot support alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ad with mock screens or video | A shown promise attracts attention from the people reached | Those people need all proposed features or will keep using the app |
| Concept page and waitlist | Visitors take a small step after seeing the offer | They will pay, migrate records, connect Accounts, or invite another Member |
| Observed prototype task | Participants understand a number or complete a proposed workflow | Bank sync works, totals are correct, or use will continue over time |
| Small assisted pilot | Participants invest effort and use a delivered result | The same value can be delivered cheaply and reliably at scale |

For example, a participant who attends a session, works through a real budgeting question, and returns for the next review supplies evidence beyond an email address. Their actions still require interpretation: a payment tests the particular offer and terms; participation in research may reflect interest in helping the researcher. Neither proves long-term retention.

For this finance product, separate attraction from trust. A mock bank connection can test whether the explanation is clear. It cannot show that someone will grant real access. A synthetic Book can test comprehension without collecting statements. The founder's statements can test financial cases; they cannot establish other people's demand.

## Design the test before buying traffic — proposed practice

Record the audience, problem, promise, artifact, requested action, price information if any, channel, budget, duration, primary metric, and the decision the result will inform. Define what counts as a qualified response and how duplicate or invalid responses will be handled. Record success, failure, and inconclusive outcomes before launch.

Treat the observed result as a result for the complete offer. Audience, channel, message, price, and timing can all change who responds. If all change between campaigns, a difference does not identify its cause. Use comparable assignment when a causal comparison matters; otherwise describe the comparison as exploratory.

Report counts and denominators alongside rates, and preserve uncertainty when the sample is small. A weak response can reflect the promise, reach, trust, or presentation. It does not establish that nobody needs the product. A high signup rate without a stated price supplies no direct evidence of willingness to pay.

Set thresholds from the decision at stake and the proposed business constraints. Do not import a generic industry percentage as a proof of demand. Keep conceptual screens clearly identified as a proposed product. Follow initial interest with an action tied to the value being tested, then decide whether a larger investment is justified.
